About Me

I am passionate about combining art, social research, and engineering to make experimental objects that emphasize materiality in interaction. My interest in material culture was sparked at Emory University, during the pursuit of my honor's thesis advised by Dr. Timothy Dowd. I discovered the field of human-robot-interaction while completing my MS at CU Boulder and designed my capstone Marshmallow Robots under the supervision of Dr. Ellen Do.

In my free time, I enjoy painting and walking in the forest.

Robotics and Design Research

At CU Boulder I collaborated with the HIRO Lab (Humans Interacting with RObots) directed by Dr. Alessandro Roncone. Here, I designed and fabricated multi-material 3D printed robot skins to contribute to ongoing research on embodied intelligence through tactile sensing. I became very familiar working with TPU, verashore, and PLA, as well as the Franka Emika Panda and Unitree H-1 Humanoid robots. I worked closely with robotics PhD students in the lab and enrolled in graduate-level robotics courses to better understand the foundations of SLAM, localization, inverse kinematics, and control systems.

Also at CU Boulder, I worked in the ACME Lab directed by Dr. Ellen Do. I had the great pleasure to assist in research by: qualitatively analyzing participatory design interview data on human-robot-interaction with elderly adults, designing and piloting an AI literacy 'puppet' to educate elementary school students on the limits of AI, and building my MS capstone project Marshmallow Robot Skins under the supervision of Dr. Ellen Do.

Social Research

At Emory University, I assisted Dr. Tracy Scott in her research on the sociology of the early space era, specifically the emergence of the role of 'astronaut' and the resulting culture within the group of 'astronaut families'. I conducted qualitative image analysis of archival photographs and magazines, documenting how visual depictions of astronauts and their families were presented to the public.

I also conducted an independent honors thesis in the department of sociology on the semiotics of local archival punk posters, which spurred my interest in approaching handwriting as data and a subject of interactive art.
