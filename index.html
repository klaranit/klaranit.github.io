With a background in sociology, art, and creative technology, I am interested in the effect of materiality on facilitating untraditional human-machine relationships. Currently, I am recontextualizing the robot body as an ephemeral, edible object through my Marshmallow Robots and exploring how food materials can be utilized to create culturally charged, computational interactions.

I am currently interested in robots and their fluctuatation between social roles of 'object', 'agent', 'worker', 'pet', and 'companion'. To provoke the limits of these borders, I create robotic prototypes and add materials that embed the robot body with a new dimension of interaction. Then I observe how this variable provokes participant interpretation, engagement, and reflection on robots. My Marshmallow Robot Skins were my first foray into this speculative HRI work, and I am interested in continuing to work with materials that bring organic, ephemeral, cyclical, and residuous qualities to the robot body.

My interest in materiality branches out to another research medium: handwriting. As opposed to the minute interferences physics and electricty can impress on a waddling marshmallow robot to bring it to 'life', human handwriting is unique for its symbolic representation of self and active engagement with an 'other' through material traces. Graffiti, personal notes, and community posters all frame relationships between authors and readers differently based on material and social context around the handwriting. This variety of relationship, interpretation, usage, and intent makes handwriting a rich medium to explore WHY people communicate through certain materials and HOW these insights can be used to develop technology that complements the subtler methods of human communication.

Research Experience

At CU Boulder I collaborated with the HIRO Lab (Humans Interacting with RObots) directed by Dr. Alessandro Roncone. Here, I designed and fabricated multi-material 3D printed robot skins to contribute to ongoing research on embodied intelligence through tactile sensing. I became very familiar working with TPU, verashore, and PLA, as well as the Franka Emika Panda and Unitree H-1 Humanoid robots. I worked closely with robotics PhD students in the lab and enrolled in graduate-level robotics courses to better understand the foundations of SLAM, localization, inverse kinematics, and control systems.

Also at CU Boulder, I worked in the ACME Lab directed by Dr. Ellen Do. I had the great pleasure to assist in research by: qualitatively analyzing participatory design interview data on human-robot-interaction with elderly adults, designing and piloting an AI literacy 'puppet' to educate elementary school students on the limits of AI, and building my MS capstone project Marshmallow Robot Skins under the supervision of Dr. Ellen Do.

At Emory University, I assisted Dr. Tracy Scott in her research on the sociology of the early space era, specifically the emergence of the role of 'astronaut' and the resulting culture within the group of 'astronaut families'. I conducted qualitative image analysis of archival photographs and magazines, documenting how visual depictions of astronauts and their families were presented to the public.
